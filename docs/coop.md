# Story co-op (work in progress)

Simon's story for 2 to 4 players on a listen server, joined through Steam's relay network: no Radmin, no port forwarding, and the players' IP addresses stay hidden. The maps are the retail ones, unmodified; everything is done in code. Single player is unchanged: none of this runs unless a game is started with `rc_coop 1` and more than one player slot.

The design and the reasons are in the research reports (`I:\COFPP\reports`, local); the bug rows are B368 to B396, B402 to B406 and B416 to B491 in [bugs.md](bugs.md).

## Playing

Every player needs the same ReCoF build and Steam running.

- **The lobby:** in the main menu choose Co-op (Beta) (B497: it replaced Join Server, Host Server and Story Co-op; B541: marked as a beta; the game menu's Co-op (Beta) opens it too). The host's lobby opens with two tabs. Game: the players in it, the game (Story co-op first, then Cry of Fear's own co-op games, B498), the difficulty (which brings its co-op rules) and, for story co-op, New game or Continue (the newest co-op save). Co-op settings: for story co-op the host's rules (how long an exit waits, how soon a dead player comes back, monster health per extra player, autosave, the skip vote, Simon or police officers), for Cry of Fear's own games two of Host Server's settings (back after dying, `sv_auto_respawn_time`; how long the game's own lobby waits, `mp_footsteps`), and each player's own voice settings. Then Invite friends, Server list (the server browser that Join Server opened), Leave and Start. Friends who accept the invite come into the lobby and see the host's choices; when the host presses Start, their games join his by themselves. A friend invited later joins the running game the same way, and a guest who left can come back with Join game. In a game, Esc and Co-op go back to the main menu and open the lobby there. `rc_lobby` prints the lobby's state.
- **Host from the console:** `rc_coop_start` (Simon's story from the start), `rc_coop_start <map> [easy|normal|hard|nightmare]` (for example `rc_coop_start c_apartment1`) or `rc_coop_continue coop_auto`; `rc_coop_menu` opens the panel. It also works as a launch option, `+rc_coop_start c_apartment1`. When Steam has given the game an address (a few seconds), the console shows `your friends join with: rc_coop_join 169.254.x.x:port`.
- **Invite:** the lobby's Invite friends, or in the game Esc and Invite Steam friends (or `rc_coop_invite`), opens Steam's own invite list in the overlay; a friend who accepts comes into the lobby, or joins directly when the host has no lobby. Friends can also use Join Game on the host in their Steam friends list, or type the `rc_coop_join` line the host's console shows.
- **Join by hand:** `rc_coop_join 169.254.x.x:port` in the console.
- **Cry of Fear's own co-op games (B498):** the lobby also hosts the co-op campaign (`cof_campaign_01`, and its parts 2 to 4), Manhunt (`cof_manhunt_campaign`), Survival 1 to 4 (`cof_suicide1` to 4) and any other map Host Server lists (the maps without `c_` in their name, so a multiplayer map a player adds shows up under its file name). They start with `rc_coop_mp <map> <back after dying> <game starts after>`: up to 4 players through Steam as in story co-op, with Cry of Fear's own multiplayer rules (`rc_coop 0`), its lobby and its police officers; that multiplayer has no saves. Leaving puts the game back to one player slot, as after story co-op (B492). The custom campaigns (The Stairway and the others) are single player campaigns and are not in the lobby.

- **Invites while the game is closed:** Steam starts the game it knows, retail Cry of Fear. The lobby's Copy Steam launch options (or `rc_coop_launchoptions`, which prints it) gives `"<ReCoF folder>\cof.exe" %command%` to paste in Steam, Cry of Fear, Properties, Launch options; Steam then starts ReCoF and it comes into the lobby (`+connect_lobby`). While the option is set, Steam's Play starts ReCoF too; clear it to play retail from Steam.
- **Status:** `rc_coop_status` shows the datagrams through Steam and the relay network's state.
- **Leaving:** a guest whose host loads a save or restarts connects again by himself (up to 10 tries). A guest who leaves (the game menu's Leave the co-op game, Disconnect, `disconnect` or `rc_coop_leave`) stays out, and `rc_coop_join` brings him back.
- **Saving:** the game saves itself as `coop_auto` once everyone is in on each new level, and as `coop_tape` when anyone uses a tape recorder (on the hardest difficulty it costs that player a tape); the host can also type `save <name>`. The host loads with `load coop_auto`, `load coop_tape` or `load <name>`; every saved player is kept for his player (matched by name, so 3 or 4 players may come back in any order), and the guests come back by themselves. A saved player whose player does not come back within 90 seconds is put away. Hosting or joining gives a player who kept the default name (Unknown) his Steam name, so the names tell the players apart.

## What the co-op does

- **Level exits wait for the group.** A player who walks into an exit waits there with "Waiting at the exit: k of N players" on everyone's screen until the whole group is in it, or until `rc_coop_exit_wait` seconds pass (default 60), then the players who are not there are brought to the exit. The host can end the wait with `rc_coop_go`. Exits fired by a door or a script do not wait.
- **Everyone keeps their things through a level change.** The engine carries every player through the single-player transition (health, weapons, inventory), as it does for Simon alone; once a player's game has loaded the new level the server sends it his HUD, weapons and screen state again, so nothing a scene hid stays hidden.
- **No bouncing back.** A player who arrives inside the exit back counts for exits only after he has stepped out of every exit once.
- **Players still loading cannot be hurt, and the level waits for them.** On a new level (or after a load) the world holds still until every player carried in has loaded, 20 seconds at most: scenes, timers and monsters wait, nobody touches a trigger, and those already in see "Waiting for the others to load...".
- **Respawn.** A player who dies comes back next to a living teammate after `rc_coop_respawn_time` seconds (default 10), with his weapons and ammo. Reviving by CPR works as in the police co-op.
- **Keys belong to the group.** A key, fuse or other story item one player picks up is in everyone's inventory; when it is used on its door or lock it is gone for everyone.
- **One code for the group.** The padlock combination and the keypad code (and the keypad it is for) are the same for every player; once one player opens the keypad, the code is used up for everyone.
- **Padlocks and puzzles are the user's.** A padlock is the player's who opened it: cancelling or solving it frees him, not the host, and a teammate cannot take it over. The bar puzzle's end gives the view back to the player looking at it; the statue puzzle's end closes everyone's statue screen.
- **Scenes take the group along.** A scene that teleports Simon teleports the whole group; an arrival room takes everyone standing in it; nobody lands inside a teammate, and players a level change brings in on one spot step apart, so the triggers where they arrive go off.
- **Cameras** already show every scene to every player (Cry of Fear's own code).
- **Skipping scenes.** The host's Enter (or `rc_cutscene_skip`) skips a scene he is watching for everyone: the game hurries through it, and the players watching it see the skip screen. A guest's Enter is his vote: everyone sees "<name> wants to skip the scene (1 of 2): Enter to agree", and once every player in the scene has pressed Enter the group skips it (`rc_coop_skip_vote 0` leaves it to the host).
- **Teammates.** No friendly fire; a player who joins comes in next to a teammate, never inside an exit, a teleport or a hurting trigger. A respawn gives back the weapons in their inventory order, each clip and the quick slots. A revived player stands on a free spot when he lay in a wall.
- **Stuck?** `rc_unstuck` in the console puts you next to a living teammate (not while down, in a scene, on a ladder or in a door; once every 10 seconds).
- **Pause.** Only the host pauses (his menu pauses everyone); a guest's menu pauses nobody.
- **Simon, not a police officer.** Simon's voice, hints, dual wield, the slow walk at low health and the map's own sky, as in single player. In story co-op the players are Simon: his body, his voice and his arms on the weapons, unless the host picks the police officers (`rc_coop_simon`); Cry of Fear's own co-op games keep their police officers. The game has no Simon model made for players, so ReCoF makes one at the first start from the game's own files (the officer's skeleton and animations with Simon's body from the cutscene model) into `models/player/simon/simon.mdl`.
- **A team wipe** brings everyone back at the level's start with their things, without restarting the map.
- **Story effects for everyone.** A weapon the map hands to Simon (the camera in the c_nightmare dream), objectives, on-screen texts, grey fades, rain changes and the phone going dead reach every player (the music already did), and so do the keypad code note, the boss bar and the end of the wheelchair ride.
- **The one who set it off.** Story entities written for one player act on the player who set them off, not the host: a button unlocked with an item, the c_basement keypad, the strangle scene, the look-at scares, the doctor duel's and the sledgehammer Book Simon's triggers.
- **Voices come from the teammate.** In story co-op a teammate's voice comes from where he stands, fades out with distance (silent at `rc_voice_range` units, 1400) and sounds muffled behind a wall (`rc_voice_wall` percent of its volume, 10). `rc_voice_3d 0` brings back the flat retail voice; `voice_showchannels 1` prints where each voice plays. The room's echo does not reach voices yet.
- **Bosses take the nearest player.** The roof boss and Purnell fight the nearest player they can see, Book Simon shoots at the nearest player in a wheelchair, and heads turn to the nearest player. A player in a scene is unseen on his own, not because a teammate is in one.
- **Players pass through each other** (Cry of Fear's own rule), and each player's game predicts it that way, so walking into a teammate does not stop you and jump you ahead; a teammate whose body surrounds your view is not drawn. Players a level change puts on one spot still set off what they stand in (c_start's opening scene after c_nightmare).
- **Invites from a game.** Accepting a friend's invite while you play takes you to the menu and opens the lobby; in that lobby's own game already, you stay.
- **Late joiners** come in with the weapons of the teammate they appear next to; the group's keys follow by themselves. A player who joins in a slot someone left starts clean.
- **Phone calls reach someone.** A story call rings a teammate with a phone when the one it was for is down or gone, rings again when its player is lost mid-call, and the story goes on after 30 seconds with nobody able to answer.
- **One exit at a time.** Two players in two exits do not restart each other's wait: the first exit counts, and no other exit goes in the same moment.
- **Places that carry one player.** The c_lake boat brings the group to its dock; the c_bridge carriage, the c_hamn door that locks behind, the walls that go up in c_down and c_fsewer1, the c_apartment1 elevator and a right photo in c_nightmare bring the group beside the player who set them off. The roofs' fall fade blacks out only the one who fell, every player gets a wheelchair in c_ending4b (a destroyed one starts the level again for the group, as single player's game over, and no save is made during the ride), the c_village3 door gives the group one ending, and c_college1's left items come back whoever goes through.
- **Held lights for everyone.** A teammate's lantern, lit flare or book laser lights the room for you too.
- **Harder with more players.** Monsters get 25% more health for each player beyond the first (at most double), the monsters the map places too; a player joining or leaving changes it, and a hurt monster stays as hurt. This takes the place of the police co-op's damage multiplier; the police co-op's hunting monster Simon never appears.

## Settings (host)

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `rc_coop` | 0 | 1 with more than one slot plays the story in co-op (set by `rc_coop_start`) |
| `rc_coop_exit_wait` | 60 | seconds an exit waits for the group; 0 changes level at once |
| `rc_coop_respawn_time` | 10 | seconds before a dead player comes back with his things; 0 keeps Cry of Fear's rule |
| `rc_coop_autosave` | 1 | saves `coop_auto` once everyone is in on each new level |
| `rc_coop_monster_health` | 25 | percent more monster health for each player beyond the first, at most double; 0 for single player's |
| `rc_coop_skip_vote` | 1 | 1 lets the guests skip a scene once every player watching it has pressed Enter; 0 only the host skips |

The game's four difficulties are the presets: `rc_coop_preset easy`, `normal`, `hard` or `nightmare` sets the difficulty and its co-op rules, and `rc_coop_preset` alone shows them. Easy: back 5 s after dying, monsters as for one player. Normal: the table above. Hard: back after 20 s, monsters 40% tougher for each friend. Nightmare: Cry of Fear's own death rule, tape recorders only (a tape each), monsters 50% tougher for each friend, and nightmare's lower stamina for everyone. Every co-op save keeps the difficulty and the rules it was played with, and loading it sets them again.

The voice options (`rc_voice_3d`, `rc_voice_range`, `rc_voice_wall`) are each player's own, and so are how you talk and the microphone (Options, Audio, Talking; the lobby's Co-op settings has Talk too): `rc_voice_mode 0` is push to talk (the voice key), `1` an open mic that sends while you speak louder than `rc_voice_open_level` percent (4) and a moment after; `rc_voice_mic` is the Windows name of the microphone to record (empty: the Windows default), used from the next map. `rc_voice_status` prints the open mic's level, the threshold and the microphone the game opened, and lists the microphones.

These are server settings: they live in the host's game, so the host's value applies to everyone and a guest cannot change it. The same holds for the movement settings (`rc_bhopcap`, `rc_hlmovement`, `rc_ducktap`), which already reach every player's prediction.

## Not done yet

- A load menu for co-op saves, and loading the last save after a team wipe.
- The per-map rules not needed so far: c_intro's logo spacing, c_subway2st1's older fuses (the group's keys are used up for everyone), the boss fights' start at their bar (players respawn during them), and the rest of the places where the story only looks at the first player (G7): sounds played "from the player".
- Matching a returning player to his saved slot by Steam ID (by name today; names are the players' Steam names).
- The voice's room echo, push to talk tuning, Simon's phone as a radio, and monsters that hear voices (decisions 13 to 18).
- A death trigger (cof_killplayer) kills the whole group, which brings everyone back at the level's start (a team wipe); single player's death is the same restart for Simon.

## Tested

On 2026-11-17 with two PCs and two Steam accounts, every packet through Valve's relays: joining, the level change c_start to c_start2 with both players' health and weapons, the exit wait and gathering, the arrival guard, respawn with weapons, a team wipe, the apartment key in c_apartment1 shared and used up for both, the shared padlock code, the teleport step-aside, Simon's voice, saving and loading with both players (a guest coming back by himself), the autosave after a level change holding both players, host-only pause, the host skipping the intro for both (on to c_nightmare, with the HUD, the opening scene and the camera for both), monster health with one and two players and after a load, a guest leaving and joining again, the single-player level change and intro skip unchanged, and the speedrun address check. A tape recorder saved the group (the host using it; loading coop_tape gave back the saved health). Later the same day: a guest using a tape recorder (loading coop_tape gave him back his saved health), the keypad code a guest learned on c_sewer2 opening the keypad for the host and used up for both, c_basement's keypad code and picture reaching the guest, the padlock on c_start (a guest opening, cancelling and solving it, the host untouched), a guest's skip vote, a guest coming back by name into his own slot after two loads, the presets kept in a save, and continuing a co-op save from the main menu (the menu and its panel seen in screenshots). Not tested: the overlay invite and Join Game clicks, three or four players, two players on different internet connections, Proton, the story entities of the later maps (bosses, puzzles, the endings), which were only built, the proximity voice, which was built and run but not heard (no microphone was recorded), and Steam's own invite and Join Game with the game closed (simulated: ReCoF started with Steam's command line from the retail folder and joined).

## For testers

- `rc_coop_dump` (host console) prints every player's health, place, weapons, inventory, codes and keypad type, whether he is seen, what keeps him from using things (phone, call, ladder, reviving, padlock), the live monsters and the padlocks.
- `rc_coop_host [direct] [nomap]` and `rc_coop_join <ip:port> [direct]` are the pieces `rc_coop_start` uses. Every packet goes through Steam's relays by default, so the players' IP addresses stay hidden; `direct` lets Steam use a direct connection too (it can be faster on one network, and it shows each player's IP address to the other).
- Starting `cof.exe` with `-rccmdfile` runs the console commands written to `rc_cmd.txt` next to `cof.exe` once a second (test scripts only).
- `rc_coop_steam.log` next to `cof.exe` logs the Steam side (no Steam IDs or addresses).
